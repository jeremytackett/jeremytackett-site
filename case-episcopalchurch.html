case study — digital transformation

Rebuilding episcopalchurch.org

Somewhere around the fourth hour of a stakeholder meeting about navigation labels, it became pretty clear the real problem wasn't design. It was that nobody — communications, IT, the dioceses, the translation team — actually agreed on what the site was for. That's usually the actual job on a project like this: not picking fonts, but getting a room full of smart people with different priorities to agree on a plan before you build anything.

The Episcopal Church's flagship website hadn't had a real structural rework in years, and it was carrying the weight of it — a decade of accumulated content, three languages that had grown up separately instead of together, and a governance model that basically didn't exist. I led the effort start to finish: information architecture, content strategy, a new design system, accessibility work, search and taxonomy, and the migration to a modern WordPress platform, all while keeping a few hundred stakeholders reasonably happy along the way.

The technical rebuild was the easy part, honestly. The harder part was building something that could actually hold up after I walked away — governance and editorial standards that a communications team without a developer on staff could maintain, and workflows that made sense for people juggling a dozen other responsibilities, not just the ones who'd been in the planning meetings.

This is the kind of work I actually love — not just building the thing, but building the conditions that let the thing keep working after I'm gone.
