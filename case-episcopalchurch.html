Rebuilding episcopalchurch.org

Visit episcopalchurch.org ↗

When we started, the honest answer to "what should this website be?" was: we didn't know yet. Not because anyone disagreed — because nobody had actually asked. Communications, IT, dioceses, individual congregations, everyday Episcopalians — nobody had ever sat all of them down, together, and asked what they actually needed this site to do for them.

So before touching a single design file, we did the unglamorous work of finding out. That's really all "information architecture" is, when you strip the jargon off it — figuring out what things should be called, where they should live, and how someone actually finds them, based on how real people think and search, not how an org chart happens to be shaped. We spent around fifty hours interviewing stakeholders and everyday users, and ran a survey that more than 1,200 Episcopalians answered — from clergy running mid-size parishes to people who'd found the church a few months earlier. That's what actually shaped episcopalchurch.org — real answers from real people, not our best guesses.

The more visible work — a new design system, accessibility improvements, migrating to a modern WordPress platform — came after that, and honestly, it was the easier part. The harder part was building something that could keep holding up after I walked away: governance and editorial standards a communications team without a developer on staff could maintain, and a structure that made sense because it was based on what actual users told us, not just what looked good in a wireframe.

It launched this week, so I don't have traffic numbers or adoption stats yet — those come with time. What I can tell you is what it took to get here: a lot of patience, a lot of listening, and a genuine belief that a church's website should feel like an invitation, not a filing cabinet.

This is the kind of work I actually love — not just building the thing, but building the conditions that let the thing keep working after I'm gone.
