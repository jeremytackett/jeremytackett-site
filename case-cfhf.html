case study — original concert series

The Concert for the Human Family

There's a particular kind of silence that happens when a room stops performing politeness and starts actually listening. I've felt it dozens of times over the years, and almost all of them happened on nights when this concert was playing.

The idea started with a simple belief: reconciliation isn't just something to talk about — it's something people can feel together, in the same room, at the same time. Music does that in a way a sermon or a strategy memo never will. So I co-created this project and concert series with GRAMMY-winning musical director and pianist Kory Caudill, building a live experience that moves across jazz, hip-hop, bluegrass, and Americana — traditions that each carry their own history of struggle, joy, and voice, woven into one shared arc rather than kept in separate lanes.

Over the past six years, that idea has become something bigger than either of us expected: more than fifty concerts, two albums with a third currently in production, and a full-scale concert film produced during COVID that went on to win both Webby and Telly Award recognition. The project keeps growing, and it's found audiences well beyond the Episcopal Church.
